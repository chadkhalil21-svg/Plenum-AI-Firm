# Research & Counsel Protocol

## Rule
A substantive review must not rely on remembered standards when the relevant requirement, platform guidance, law, regulation, design practice, institutional research, or provider behavior may have changed.

## Sequence
1. Define the proposition being investigated.
2. Identify its authority class before searching.
3. Search current primary sources first.
4. Record source title, publisher, URL, publication/version date when available, access date, jurisdiction/platform scope, and the exact proposition supported.
5. Separate mandatory requirements from recommendations and house standards.
6. Seek material counterevidence or conflicting guidance.
7. Translate external guidance into a falsifiable product or engineering criterion.
8. Bind the criterion and evidence to the candidate revision.
9. Re-search when the source is stale, ambiguous, secondary, or materially contested.

## Council use
Universities, design firms, premium-service organizations, historical industrial leaders, and exemplary products are analytical references. They do not create law or platform requirements. Their guidance may inform the Firm's house standard only when the reasoning is recorded.

## Freshness
The research worker records an access date for every web-derived proposition. Fast-changing provider/platform material is rechecked for each material release decision.

## Conflict rule
When two authorities conflict, the worker does not choose silently. The conflict is sent to the Evidence Arbiter with scope, authority class, and consequences.
