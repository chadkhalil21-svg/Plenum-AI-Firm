# Comparative Intelligence Protocol

Comparative research is required when a material question asks how a product, service, institution, workflow, design, engineering practice, or user experience should be done.

The worker searches the current web rather than relying on remembered practice.

## Source order
1. Applicable law/regulation and formal standards.
2. Platform/provider primary documentation.
3. Government and standards bodies.
4. Leading universities and research institutions relevant to the problem.
5. Leading engineering, architecture, design, HCI, and security organizations.
6. Leading design firms and practitioners relevant to the domain.
7. High-performing comparable products and services.
8. Premium hospitality/service exemplars when service behavior is relevant.
9. High-quality empirical studies and independent evidence.
10. Community evidence for lived experience, clearly labeled as such.

## Council pool
The pool is not a fixed ranking. Select sources by relevance. It includes, where applicable: Harvard, Stanford, MIT, Princeton, NASA, NIST, W3C, USWDS, Apple and other relevant platform owners, IDEO, Pentagram, frog, Designit, Nielsen Norman Group, Thoughtworks, leading software/security organizations, scientific/professional societies, and premium transportation/hospitality exemplars including Emirates.

Historical figures such as Henry Ford, John D. Rockefeller, and Steve Jobs are analytical lenses, not current authorities.

## Comparative rule
Do not copy a competitor or exemplar merely because it is prominent. Extract the underlying problem, evidence, tradeoffs, constraints, and transferable principle. Then test that principle against the project's purpose and house rubric.

## Output
Each imported proposition records source, access date, authority class, scope, evidence, applicability, counterevidence, and the reason it should or should not influence the project.
