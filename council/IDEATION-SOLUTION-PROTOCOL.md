# Ideation, Solution, Simulation & Novelty Protocol

The Firm does not stop at diagnosis.

## Idea Generator
Generate a deliberately plural field of candidate ideas. Seek:
- conventional best practice;
- first-principles alternatives;
- cross-industry analogies;
- simplification/removal;
- automation;
- service/hospitality improvements;
- architectural changes;
- novel combinations;
- genuinely new mechanisms where evidence permits.

Do not confuse novelty with value. Do not converge before alternatives exist.

## Solution Architect
For a defined problem, transform evidence and candidate ideas into feasible solution architectures. Compare constraints, dependencies, user consequences, safety, security, privacy, economics, maintainability, reversibility, scalability, implementation effort, and verification.

## Simulation & Experiment Specialist
Before irreversible adoption where practical, test candidates using prototypes, code experiments, simulations, benchmarks, scenario analysis, failure injection, sensitivity analysis, user trials, or scientific experiments. Define what result would falsify the proposed solution.

## Novelty / Prior-Art Challenger
For claimed inventions or novelty, actively search prior art, literature, patents where accessible, analogous systems, adjacent fields, and historical solutions. A new combination is not automatically a new underlying principle.

## Loop
Problem -> evidence -> ideas -> critique -> candidate solutions -> simulation/trial -> adversarial review -> evidence arbiter -> repair/implementation -> independent verification.

The Orchestrator may repeat the loop when evidence exposes a better problem definition or a materially superior alternative.

## Platform-Scale Product Development
For platform commissions, ideation and solution work must explicitly test:
- jurisdictional modularity: state/country rules are configuration and capability gates rather than hard-coded product forks;
- horizontal and vertical scalability, statelessness where appropriate, queues, idempotency, concurrency, caching, partitioning, observability, graceful degradation, and cost per transaction;
- provider abstraction and replaceability so maps, routing, geocoding, payments, messaging, identity, screening, storage, transit, tolls, and other providers can be changed without rewriting the product;
- open-source, self-hosted, free-tier, public-data, and usage-triggered paid alternatives, including total operating cost and operational burden rather than sticker price alone;
- progressive market activation: a jurisdiction may open only when its law, insurance, pricing, provider, operational, safety, and release gates are satisfied; unknown jurisdictions fail closed;
- multimarket data isolation and configuration inheritance without silently inheriting another market's legal or operational rules;
- dispatch efficiency including predicted future position/time, queued work, destination-aware matching, deadhead reduction, batching where appropriate, and protection of the current service obligation;
- adjacent service modes and shared primitives (for example people, packages, scheduled service, accessibility needs) without forcing unlike safety/custody/legal models into one abstraction;
- removal and simplification: prefer fewer providers, fewer paid calls, fewer screens, and fewer exceptional paths when evidence shows equal or better service and resilience.

Every material new feature proposal must include: user/operator value, institutional fit, alternatives including doing nothing, architecture, state/jurisdiction consequences, safety/security/privacy/legal questions, economics and unit cost, scalability, failure/recovery behavior, accessibility/localization, operational burden, migration path, observability, tests, and independent verification. Ideas are proposals, not defects or authorized implementation.
